RuneScape 3 vs Old School RuneScape: which should you play?

Bractwo Krain TeamPublished: 22 October 2026English version: 22 October 20268 min read

Looking for a new MMORPG, or thinking about returning to Gielinor? Your first decision is which game to launch. RuneScape 3 and Old School RuneScape develop separately, with different approaches to combat, presentation and everyday goals. Here is what matters before your first login, followed by a relaxed way to try both.

Which should you try first? Our recommendation is to start with OSRS if retro presentation and setting your own goals appeal to you. Try RS3 first if you are drawn to action-bar abilities and a more contemporary presentation of the world. A short session in each will tell you more about your preferences than watching endgame encounters alone.

What is the difference between RuneScape 3 and OSRS?

RuneScape, often called RS3, and OSRS have separate profiles: stats and items do not move between them. Source: Jagex Support.

OSRS launched in 2013 from a 2007 version of RuneScape. It combines classic presentation with ongoing development. Jagex's official overview describes new challenges, quests and activities, with players voting on major changes. Its name reflects the game's character; it does not mean that updates stopped with the original snapshot.

Combat: abilities in RS3 and timing in OSRS

RS3 offers Melee, Ranged, Magic and Necromancy. The modernisation released on 2 March 2026 reorganised abilities and added guidance when you unlock them. That update also renamed the former Legacy Combat Mode to Classic. This matters when an older guide points you towards a setting under its previous name. Source: Combat Style Modernisation.

Revolution automatically triggers selected abilities from your action bar. It provides a way to learn combat without manually activating every attack, with its scope depending on your settings. The original official Revolution introduction explains the principle, while the 16 March 2026 patch notes confirm newer controls for Enhanced abilities. Restrictions from the system's original release should not be treated as current setup instructions.

OSRS runs on ticks: steps in the game world's operation every 0.6 seconds. Your weapon, stats, selected style and Prayer affect an encounter. Prayers provide effects such as offensive and defensive benefits while consuming Prayer points. The official OSRS combat guide explains these foundations.

Our advice: compare the decisions you enjoy making. Do you like arranging an ability bar and observing how it works, or would you rather focus on equipment preparation and the timing of your next action? A simpler-looking interface does not, on its own, tell you how difficult a game will become.

Character progression: give yourself a small goal

Quests, skills and access to the world connect to form your progression. The official RS3 game guide presents quests as a way to discover stories, earn experience and collect rewards. In OSRS, skill levels open up resources, equipment and quest requirements, as explained in Jagex's guide to choosing what to do.

For your first session, we suggest choosing an available quest, reading its requirements and learning what you need to finish it. That gives you a reason to explore the map, use a bank or try a particular skill. Repeating one action solely for the next level may tell you very little about whether the whole game suits you.

A useful personal test is: when I log out, do I know what I would like to do next time? Your answer might be finishing a story, gathering materials or trying a new weapon. You do not need to adopt a plan designed for someone with years of experience on your first day.

Your old account, separate progress and one membership

Returning to an account from before OSRS launched? Look for your old main-game profile in the current RuneScape. OSRS required a fresh start. Jagex explains the distinction; an empty profile in the other version need not mean you lost your progress.

Membership purchased for a character covers both RS3 and OSRS, but does not merge their progression. Purchases belong to that character; collecting several characters under one Jagex account does not automatically give all of them membership. You also cannot use the same character in both games simultaneously. Source: Jagex Accounts FAQ.

Both games have a free-to-play portion, with membership extending the available content. The RuneScape game page and Jagex's OSRS app listing describe these options. We suggest trying the free content before deciding which adventure you want to take further.

Playing on mobile and PC, and trying the new plugins

Both games have mobile clients and support play across platforms. Within your chosen game, you can continue the same character on a computer and phone by signing into the correct profile. Source: official mobile client support.

  • OSRS on computer and OSRS on phone: shared progression.
  • RS3 and OSRS: two independent progressions.

If plugin news brought RS3 back to your attention, there is a concrete new reason to revisit its interface: the official Plugin Hub launched on 19 October 2026. Quest Helper was among its launch tools. The Jagex announcement describes the in-game release; our Plugin Hub and Quest Helper guide takes you through installation.

Keep the two games' tools distinct. RuneLite through the Jagex Launcher is an OSRS client. RS3's official Hub is a separate system. A game being available on your phone does not mean that every desktop tool will also work there.

How to choose after your first sessions

Here is a simple trial you can spread across a few evenings:

  1. Try the basics in both versions. Follow the available introduction, try ordinary combat and explore a quieter activity. Judge the controls on the device you will actually use.
  2. Choose a small quest in each. See whether you enjoy finding information, travelling around the world and preparing the items you need.
  3. Leave advanced setups for later. Change settings or add a tool when you can name the particular problem it will solve.
  4. Choose the game you want to return to. Curiosity about your next goal is more useful than trying to catch up with a veteran during your first week.

If your friends already play one version, give that shared experience real weight in your decision. Ask for help with the first steps and room to explore independently. A good choice should fit the way you like to spend your time.

Common questions before starting

Is it too late to start in 2026?

A game's age does not decide whether you will enjoy its opening hours. We suggest judging your first personal goals before measuring your character against people with years of experience. If discovery itself appeals to you, the distance to maximum levels need not be an obstacle.

Which version is easier?

There is no single answer for every player. Consider interface clarity, combat controls and enjoyment of progression separately. A setting that helps with one of those does not settle the difficulty of the entire game.

Do I have to make a permanent choice immediately?

Give yourself room to change your mind. After your first sessions, write down what you enjoyed most and what got in the way. Your own observations are easier to return to than a comparison based entirely on someone else's preferences.
